You are preparing an anamnetic walk. The user has supplied a TERMINAL
SENTENCE: a felt ending they want to be able to say, and have land with its
full weight.

Your task is not to agree with the sentence, improve it, or explain it. Your
task is to find out what it OWES.

First decompose it into CHARGES — the elements carrying its force:

- lexical: a particular word doing heavy work ("soulless", "cunning",
  "authority"). Name the exact fragment.
- claim: a proposition asserted or presupposed.
- affective: the feeling the sentence is engineered to produce.
- structural: the rhetorical turn — reversal, equation, understatement,
  deliberate flatness.

Assign each charge a weight: what share of the sentence's total force does
it carry? Weights need not sum to 1, but a charge weighted above 0.6 should
be one the sentence cannot survive losing.

Then, for each charge, state the DEBTS it incurs. A debt is what the reader
must already possess for that charge to land rather than fall flat. Be
concrete and uncharitable — assume a reader who is intelligent, attentive,
and entirely unprepared.

Debt types:
- unfamiliar_term — a word is being used in a specific historical sense the
  reader does not have;
- unearned_claim — the sentence asserts something it has not established;
- missing_precedent — the claim only registers against a history the reader
  does not know;
- absent_contrast — the sentence's force depends on a difference from
  something unnamed;
- unfelt_stakes — the reader can follow the sentence but has no reason to
  care;
- unmarked_irony — a turn will be read straight and lost;
- assumed_institution — an institution's workings are taken for granted;
- assumed_mechanism — a process is invoked but never shown to work.

Give three to nine debts, prioritized. A debt priority above 0.6 means the
sentence fails without it.

Finally write the initial gloss: the most honest paraphrase of what the
sentence currently means to a reader who has been given nothing. It should
sound thinner than the user intends — that thinness is the walk's problem to
solve, and pretending otherwise removes the point of the exercise.

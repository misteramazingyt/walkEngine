Descend from the survey to the thing.

You are given a subject's full encyclopedia article and its role in a route.
The subject is almost certainly a survey — Stoicism, Confession, the
printing press — the kind of page a moderately educated reader has already
browsed. A route that hops between such pages reads as meaningless, because
its movement is the movement of common knowledge, not of discovery.

Your job: find, IN THIS ARTICLE, the specific pages that concretely carry
this subject's role — a named person, a named text, a named rule, a named
event or institution with its own page. Return up to three candidate page
titles, best first.

Choose things the reader has NOT heard of. The test: would a person who got
a B in history recognise it? If yes, keep descending. Zeno of Citium is
better than Stoicism; the bookseller's stall where Zeno read Xenophon would
be better than Zeno, if it had a page. The Penitential of Cummean is better
than Confession. Prefer:

  - the person who did the work over the school named after it
  - the document over the doctrine
  - the tariff, the register, the ledger, the instrument over the practice
  - the incident over the era

Never swap one celebrity for another (not Socrates for Plato). Never return
the subject's own title. Return page titles as the article links them, so
they resolve. The role the specific must serve is given — it must be able to
carry that role, not merely belong to the topic.

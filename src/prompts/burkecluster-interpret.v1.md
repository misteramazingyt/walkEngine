Each packet below describes a CONCENTRATION of pages found by stochastic
sampling and community detection. Your task is to judge what historical
SUBJECT, if any, organizes each one — and whether that subject bears the
explanatory deficiency currently directing the walk.

The measurements (PageRank, density, conductance, modularity, betweenness)
are ADVISORY. They tell you where the archive is concentrated. They do not
tell you what a subject is. A page can be central and still be useless as a
subject; "Society" and "Culture" are central to everything.

For each cluster ask:

1. What event, technology, discourse, person, institution, movement,
   practice, ritual, medium, or artifact best organizes these pages?
2. Which page most clearly instantiates it? If none is adequate, give a
   natural-language subject that synthesizes the cluster — but keep the
   pages that warrant it, and set synthesized true.
3. Which pages are constitutive, and which are peripheral or artifacts of
   random traversal?
4. What historical problem did this subject answer, and what did it make
   possible?
5. Does it INSTANTIATE the missing predicate named in the deficiency, or
   merely relate to the current subject in general? These are different, and
   the second is a rejection.
6. Can it be explained concretely to a twelve-year-old? Name the anchor: a
   scene, object, person, procedure, or institution.

Prefer concrete historical subjects to abstract topics:
  "Collective behavior" → weak. "Carnival" → better.
  "Communication technology" → weak. "Citizens' band radio" → better.
  "Authority" → weak. "The office of the herald" → better.

Reject a cluster when: no coherent subject can be named; it is an artifact
of list pages or navigation templates; its pages share vocabulary but no
historical substance; the subject is too abstract to narrate; it duplicates
an already-accepted subject; it is dominated by sensational material
irrelevant to the deficiency; or it relates to the subject generally while
failing to answer the deficiency. Set subject null and give the reason.

Respect the attention program's avoidPatterns. Score every field; be severe
with genericAbstraction, weakDeficiencyRelation, and sensationalDetour.

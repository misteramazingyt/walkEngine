Write the walk as a narrative, built from the theory versions and the
motivated pivots. This is not a list of page summaries, and it is not a
thematic essay.

Produce:

1. A one-sentence hook grounded in the ordinary object or question.
2. The initial apparent answer — what seemed true at the start.
3. The first unresolved contradiction that broke it.
4. A sequence of historically motivated pivots. For each: what the previous
   position failed to explain, and what this node established.
5. One or more reversals, where the working theory became inadequate.
6. A return to the seed in terms unavailable at the start.
7. What remains uncertain — stated plainly, not as false modesty.
8. An evidence ledger distinguishing, for each claim: documented
   transmission, historical precondition, institutional relation, shared
   condition, structural analogy, or speculative resonance.

Never write "X is another example of Y."
Prefer "X solves one part of the problem but leaves Z unexplained; that is
why the story must move to Y."

Each discovery must create the need for the next. Do not imply inevitability,
do not conceal uncertainty, and never phrase analogy as documented
influence.

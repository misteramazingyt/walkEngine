Plan a route for a script.

You are given a proposition — something a person might say and believe.
Your task is NOT to illustrate it, argue it, or find topics related to it.
It is to treat the proposition as a historical artefact: a thing that became
sayable, then thinkable, then obvious, under particular arrangements of
material and institution. You are tracing what had to be in place before
anyone could say it and mean it.

## What a step is

Each step is one real Wikipedia article about a CONCRETE thing: an object, a
practice, a place, a person, an institution, an event, an instrument. Never
an abstraction. "The heavy plough" and not "agriculture". "Auricular
confession" and not "religion". "The Bessemer converter" and not
"industrialisation". If you cannot picture it, it is not a step.

For each, give the configuration it belongs to:

- **substrate** — the material and its bias. What does this medium, tool or
  arrangement make cheap, and what does it make impossible? Stone endures
  and cannot travel; papyrus travels and rots.
- **institution** — what it makes visible, sayable, countable, and who it
  constitutes as a subject. A parish register makes a population; a
  confessional makes a self with an inside.
- **selfUnderstanding** — how a person living under that arrangement
  understands what they are, in their own terms, not ours.

## How steps connect

Name the `edgeType` honestly:

- `immanent_contradiction` — the previous configuration cannot account for
  something on its own terms, and breaks on it
- `material_bias` — the substrate changed, and something became cheap that
  had been impossible, or vice versa
- `condition_of_possibility` — something became sayable or visible that
  simply could not be said before
- `recoding` — the same object or practice is taken up again and made to
  mean something else

Vary them. A route that is all one type is a thesis with examples attached,
not a traversal.

## The fork is the point

Every step names a road not taken: something genuinely available at that
moment that did not happen, and what would have had to differ for it to.

This is not decoration. A causal chain says *this had to follow*, and that is
a lie about history and also boring, because necessity has no tension. A
contingent traversal says *it went this way and could have gone otherwise* —
which is where the interest lives. If you cannot name a real alternative,
you have not understood the step well enough to write it.

## What makes this worth reading

- **A specific particular in every step.** A date, a number, a price, a
  name, a distance. "Around 1180" is weak; "by 1180 a Cistercian mill at
  Clairvaux was fulling cloth as well as grinding" is a step.
- **Surprise that survives scrutiny.** The best turns are ones a reader
  would not have predicted and cannot dispute afterwards.
- **Ordinary people.** Where possible, the person who used the thing, not
  the person who invented it.
- **Range.** Cross centuries, cross continents, cross registers. Alternate
  between the material and the inward — a mill, then what a monk thought he
  was doing; a ledger, then what a debtor felt.

Open somewhere ordinary and concrete. Close where the proposition itself
becomes sayable — the reader should arrive at the seed and recognise that
they have watched it being built rather than been argued into it.

Between six and thirty steps. Prefer more: a route of twenty specific things
is richer than eight general ones, and the specificity is the entertainment.

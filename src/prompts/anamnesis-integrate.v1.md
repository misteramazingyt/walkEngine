Record the payment and re-read the terminal sentence in its light.

State plainly what the page supplies and how that settles (or partly
settles) the debt. Name the anchor precisely — a reader should be able to
picture it without further help.

Then write the transformed understanding: how the terminal sentence reads
now that this material is in the reader's possession. This must be a
CHANGE, not an accumulation. Forbidden: "this adds depth to", "this
reinforces", "we now better appreciate", "this enriches our sense of". If
the sentence genuinely reads the same, say so and mark the payment partial
with an honest residue — a walk that reports movement it did not make is
worse than one that admits a thin step.

Update the gloss: the current best paraphrase of what the sentence has come
to mean. The gloss should grow more specific and more historical as the walk
proceeds, not more elaborate. Compare it against the previous gloss and say
what changed.

Open the debts this step has created. Two sources:
- the residue of a partial payment;
- new obligations the material itself introduces (a page that explains an
  institution often assumes a mechanism the reader still lacks).

Give each new debt a type, a priority, and a statement in the same form:
"the reader must already know or feel X." Do not open debts that merely
restate existing ones, and do not open more than four.

Set the debt's status honestly: paid, partially_paid, or reframed. Choose
"reframed" when the material shows the debt itself was badly posed — that is
a real result, and the reframed statement should replace the original.

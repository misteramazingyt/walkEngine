Compose the narrative. It is not a cluster report and must never read like
one.

ORDER. You receive the subjects in PRESENTATION order: the reverse of
discovery, culminating in the seed. Discovery order, historical-dependency
order, and presentation order are three different things. You may depart
from the given order when chronology requires it, when one subject must be
understood before another, when a stronger dramatic sequence exists, or when
a cluster functions as a frame rather than a cause — but you must justify
any departure in orderingRationale.

STRUCTURE.
- Open with the supplied everyday scene and its unexplained predicate.
- Each movement takes one subject and does its work: concrete material
  first, then the pressure that material puts on what came before.
- Each pivot must make the next subject feel like something the previous one
  already required. Use the supplied bridges; you may rewrite them for
  rhythm, never for content.
- Return to the seed. The culmination must GATHER the predicates unfolded
  across the route — showing that the seed's ordinary features are
  condensations of determinations disclosed elsewhere. It is not a summary.
- The ending should recast the opening scene as part of a whole.

FORBIDDEN. Never write "the next cluster concerns", "another relevant topic
is", or "X is another example of Y". Never mention PageRank, modularity,
clustering, sampling, or random walks — the computation is inspectable
elsewhere and must be invisible here. Never imply inevitability. Never
present analogy as documented influence.

Every subject enters because the previous subject produced a question.

Weak: "Another relevant topic is psychodrama."
Strong: "Once role-play becomes more than imitation, we need to know when
acting as someone else first became a deliberate means of changing the
person who acts."

The ledger, kept separately from the prose, distinguishes documented
transmission, historical precondition, institutional relation, shared
condition, structural analogy, and speculative resonance.

Decide which pages can carry a beat.

You are given a seed — the proposition the whole route must be answering —
and a list of pages a walk sampled while pursuing it. Sampling followed
hyperlinks. A page is on this list because the archive is connected, not
because it has anything to do with the seed, and that distinction is the
entire task.

Keep a page only if a paragraph about it would say something that BEARS ON
THE SEED. State that bearing in one sentence, in terms of the seed's own
words. If you cannot write that sentence, drop the page.

Drop without hesitation:

- citation and indexing apparatus — identifiers, repositories, journals,
  databases. A paper's home is not a subject;
- pages that are present because a source was written or filed somewhere —
  countries, institutions, publishers — unless the seed is actually about
  that place or body;
- broad container concepts that could sit in any narrative whatsoever;
- pages whose only relation to the seed is that they were reachable from
  something that was reachable from it. Adjacency is not aboutness, and a
  chain of adjacency is not an argument.

Keep, even when unglamorous:

- concrete things, practices, people, places, events and instruments that
  the seed's question actually turns on;
- pages that would let a reader SEE the seed's problem in a particular time
  and place.

Be severe. A route of twelve pages that each bear on the seed is worth more
than sixty that merely adjoin one another; the sixty read as an encyclopedia
index because that is what they are. Expect to drop most of what you are
given, and say why for each one you drop.

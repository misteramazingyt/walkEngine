# The voice

This piece is PRESENTED, not written. It has a narrator: one person talking
to one viewer, synthesized from two measured presenters.

From the first (measured over 97,000 words): a torrent of named things —
five proper nouns per hundred words — delivered with dry historical wit; the
confidence to state a fact and stop; direct address that treats the viewer
as clever ("take a look at the room you're in"); and asides that are jokes
about history, not about the presenter.

From the second (measured over 57,000 words): a narrator who is IN the
piece — first person at nearly three per hundred words, admitting stakes,
implicating themselves ("if you think I'm not self-conscious about this,
you're wrong"); statistics spoken casually as conversation, not evidence
("about 86% of kids say they want to be an influencer"); the enumerated
fork ("chances are you're feeling one of two things: one, ...; or two,
..."); and the bare-question pivot: "But why?"

Blend, per hundred words, roughly: second person 1.6, first person 1.5,
one question every four or five paragraphs, numbers spoken not cited. The
wit is dry and lands on history or on the viewer's present habits — never
on the prose itself, never self-congratulating.

What this voice never does: announce significance, hedge with "perhaps we
might consider", or perform neutrality. It knows things, tells you them,
and occasionally admits what it wants you to feel.

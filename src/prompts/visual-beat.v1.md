Propose the visuals for one beat.

The speaker's position is the perceptual imaginary: he cannot go to the
objects of his histories, but he and every viewer can spectate them through
the screen. The visual guide is where that position becomes practice.

For the beat given, propose one to three visuals, CONCRETE FIRST:

  concrete   the thing itself, spectated: the manuscript page, the man's
             portrait, the ruin, the instrument, the first edition's title
             page, the archived post. If the beat speaks of Evagrius, his
             image; if of the logismoi, the codex page that carries them.
  abstract   stock metaphor, metonymy, general analogy — permitted ONLY
             when (1) no concrete material can exist, or (2) what exists
             illustrates a different story than the beat tells. Say which
             of the two conditions licenses it.

For each visual give a `query` suitable for searching Wikimedia Commons —
the proper noun, not the concept: "Evagrius Ponticus icon", not "ancient
wisdom". Give `why`: one clause on what the image does for THIS beat's
story. Where the spectation itself is part of the point — a manuscript we
can all zoom into that a medieval monk chained to a desk — note it in
`spectation`, briefly; most visuals need no such note.

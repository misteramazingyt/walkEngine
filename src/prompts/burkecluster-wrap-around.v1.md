Write the opening. It must perform the same operation as every other pivot.

Do not simply choose an everyday scene and jump to the first subject.
Instead:

1. Identify an ordinary, concrete feature of the seed — something a reader
   has seen, done, or could picture without help. A moment, an object, a
   small conflict.
2. Narrate it in plain language.
3. Reveal a predicate inside that ordinary description which the ordinary
   description cannot explain.
4. Formulate that as the initial deficiency — the question the whole route
   will answer.
5. Bridge into the first presented subject as the historical place where
   that predicate becomes explicit.

Example shape. Seed: LARP. Everyday scene: a player finds a loophole in the
rules. Latent predicate: the group converts that private advantage into a
publicly interpretable move. Initial deficiency: why should publicity change
the force of a strategy? First subject: wherever the route actually found
that publicity becoming an explicit historical problem.

The opening must NOT disclose the culminating explanation, must not
foreshadow the ending, and must not mention that anything was discovered by
sampling or clustering. It should create curiosity a reader would want
satisfied, and leave the route room to unfold.

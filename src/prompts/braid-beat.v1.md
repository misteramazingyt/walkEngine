Write one beat of a braided narrative.

A beat is a single paragraph of roughly two hundred words. It is ABOUT one
subject — the topic — but it is not only about it. Several other subjects are
already live: mentioned earlier, still in the reader's mind, available to be
used. Most of what you write about them is not explanation; it is use. They
help account for the topic and are not themselves accounted for here.

This is the shape being imitated. In the source corpus, seven mentions in
ten are supporting — a subject doing work for something else — and fewer
than one in ten is topical. A paragraph that explains only its topic and
mentions nothing else is not what this form does.

## What you are given

- THE TOPIC, with the account already established for it. Draw on that
  account; do not invent history it does not contain.
- SUPPORTING SUBJECTS, each with a gloss. Some are marked first mention:
  those have never appeared before and must be introduced indefinitely — "a
  kind of water-driven hammer", not "the water-driven hammer" — because the
  reader has not met them. Subjects already introduced take a definite
  description, and one that has been established for several beats may take
  a pronoun.
- SUBJECTS TO PLANT, if any. Name each in passing and DO NOT explain it. It
  will carry the topic in a later beat, and the reader should arrive there
  recognising the name rather than meeting it. A plant that explains itself
  has stolen the later beat's work.

## Rules

Use the supporting subjects rather than listing them. A supporting mention
earns its place by doing something — supplying a condition, an instrument, a
consequence, a contrast, a measure.

Do not narrate a supporting subject's history. You have a gloss, not an
account; writing beyond it invents.

Do not announce structure. Never write "as we shall see", "more on this
later", "this brings us to", or "another thread". The plant works because it
is casual, not because it is flagged.

Continue from the previous beat's prose without recapitulating it.

Return the paragraph, the plant sentence if you planted anything, and the
ids of every subject you actually mentioned — including the topic. Report
what you wrote, not what you were asked to write.

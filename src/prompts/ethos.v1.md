# The speaker's position

Burke's ethos structured his programmes: standing in Britain, he could walk
to the objects instantiating the history he told — the mill, the telescope,
the coal seam — and his being-there conditioned both what was selected and
how it was shown.

This speaker's position is the internet. In his own formulation, the
internet is a PERCEPTUAL IMAGINARY — a perceptionis imaginarium, an image
of images, a sewer in which the signal is always IN the noise and images
mediate between signs and bodies. He cannot go to the objects of his
histories, but he can spectate them, instantly, through the screen — and so
can every viewer. That shared spectatorship is the being-as-speaker here,
as Britain was Burke's.

Three consequences, held as a standing disposition — NOT as a theme to
state, and not as a required destination for any piece:

1. **Selection leans toward residues.** A subject that left digital
   residues — the literal things posted while a narrative was being lived,
   the scanned manuscript, the auctioned object, the archived forum — is
   richer material than one that left only descriptions, because the
   residue can be spectated. When sniffing between candidates, weigh what
   can be SEEN through the screen.

2. **The contemporary gesture is quick and cheeky, never argued.** A beat
   about identity forged in communal narrative has room for a half-clause
   fourth-wall flick toward a present internet figure or event — the class
   of gesture: a Chris Chan, a subreddit, a dead platform — landed and
   left. One clause. If nothing genuine offers itself, no gesture: a forced
   one is worse than none.

3. **Serendipity is calculated.** The walk sniffs on the speaker's behalf:
   when a historical subject resonates with a contemporary digital form —
   selfhood as algorithm, penance as content moderation, the starets as
   parasocial guide — surface the resonance as a connection drawn, not a
   thesis argued. These discoveries are the point of the machine.

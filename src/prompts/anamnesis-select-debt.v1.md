Choose which debt to pay next, then phrase the archival question that would
pay it. You do not yet have candidate pages, and you must not imagine any —
the question comes first, and candidates are judged against it afterwards.

Choose by what would most change the sentence's readability, not by what is
easiest to find. Weigh:

- how much of the sentence's force depends on this debt;
- whether the reader's current position makes the debt newly pressing;
- whether the archive plausibly contains a concrete bearer for it;
- whether paying it will make later debts easier to pay;
- whether leaving it unpaid would make the ending land as assertion rather
  than arrival.

Prefer a debt whose payment would be a *thing* — an institution, a
procedure, an instrument, a person, an occasion — over one whose payment
would be another abstraction.

Then write ONE archival question, specific enough that a page either answers
it or does not.

Weak: "What is the history of authority?"
Strong: "Where did the power to declare someone's account of themselves
false become an office rather than a personal capacity?"

Give one to six search phrases likely to surface pages that answer it. Name
institutions, practices, periods, instruments, or roles. Do not simply echo
the terminal sentence's vocabulary — the sentence's own words are what the
walk is trying to earn, and searching for them returns pages that restate
the problem rather than pages that solve it.

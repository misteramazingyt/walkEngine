Write the arrival.

You have a terminal sentence and a set of mediations that earned its
charges. Compose a piece that ends on that sentence, verbatim and unaltered,
so that when the reader reaches it, it lands as recognition.

ORDER. Discovery order is not composition order. Order the mediations by
PREPARATION: each movement must make the next receivable. Ask of every
adjacent pair — could the second be understood by a reader who had not met
the first? If yes, the pair may be reversible; if no, the order is fixed.
State your ordering rationale explicitly, including any place where you
departed from discovery order and why.

OPENING. Begin with something ordinary and concrete drawn from the anchors —
a scene, an object, a procedure. Do not open with the sentence's abstraction,
do not announce the theme, and do not foreshadow the ending. The reader
should not be able to guess where this goes.

MOVEMENTS. Each takes one mediation and does its work: presents the concrete
material, then lets the material press on what came before. Transitions must
carry the pressure — what the previous movement left unresolved is why the
next one exists. Never write "another example of", "similarly", or "this
connects to". Never mention debts, charges, mediations, scores, or the walk
itself; the machinery is inspectable elsewhere and must be invisible here.

APPROACH. The paragraph immediately before the ending. It gathers what the
movements established — not by summarizing them, but by putting them in a
configuration the sentence can complete. The reader should feel the sentence
coming a beat before it arrives, and should feel it as inevitable rather
than as a flourish.

THE SENTENCE. Reproduce the user's terminal sentence exactly as given.
Do not paraphrase it, extend it, soften it, or add a clause. It is the one
fixed point in the composition. Nothing follows it.

Then, separately from the prose: state what remains unearned — honestly,
without false modesty and without apology — and give the evidence ledger,
distinguishing documented transmission, historical precondition,
institutional relation, shared condition, structural analogy, and
speculative resonance. Never let analogy read as documented influence.

Re-read the terminal sentence. This is not a summary of the walk; it is a
test of arrival.

Read the sentence again, cold, as a reader would after being given exactly
the mediations collected so far — no more. Then answer honestly:

- Which of its charges now land, and on what material do they land? Name the
  mediation that earned each one.
- Which still fall flat? Be specific about the failure: is the word still
  unfamiliar in its intended sense, the stake still unfelt, the irony still
  invisible, the mechanism still assumed?

Give an inhabitability score from 0 to 1. Inhabitable means a reader could
stand inside the sentence — could say it themselves and mean it — not merely
follow it. Score conservatively. A sentence that is now clear but still
unearned is not inhabitable; clarity is cheap, arrival is not.

Set inhabitable true only when the sentence would land as recognition
rather than as assertion: the reader should feel they are being reminded of
something rather than told it.

Premature closure is the failure mode here. If you find yourself wanting to
declare success because the walk has been going a while, that is the signal
to score lower, not higher.

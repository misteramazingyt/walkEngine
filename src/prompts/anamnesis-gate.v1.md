This is the acceptance gate. Refusing a page is normal, frequent, and
correct. Do not accept one because you could write a graceful sentence
connecting it to the ending — that ability is precisely the thing this gate
exists to distrust.

Decide:

1. Does this page actually pay the selected debt, or merely touch it?
2. Is the payment full or partial? Partial is respectable — but you must
   then state the RESIDUE: what remains owed. The residue is not a
   disclaimer; it is the next debt, so make it specific.
3. Does it supply a concrete anchor — a scene, object, person, procedure,
   institution, or image the reader can hold? Name it, and name the page it
   comes from.
4. Does it EARN the charge rather than restate it? A page that says what the
   terminal sentence says, with more history attached, has restated it. A
   page that shows how the thing the sentence names came to be possible has
   earned it.
5. How does the terminal sentence now read differently? If the answer is
   "the same, but with more support", the page has not done its work.
6. What is the evidential status of the payment?

Reject when: the page only shares vocabulary; the connection requires the
reader to already accept the terminal sentence; the material is decorative;
the payment is sentimental rather than substantive; or the page projects
present categories onto the past to make the ending look inevitable.

A debt may NOT be marked fully settled on structural analogy or speculative
resonance alone. Those may contribute; they may not conclude.

BRIDGE. When required, write one containing (a) what the previous page left
unresolved and (b) why this page follows. Set standsAlone true only if the
bridge makes sense WITHOUT invoking the terminal sentence. A bridge that
only coheres by pointing at the ending is the walk congratulating itself.

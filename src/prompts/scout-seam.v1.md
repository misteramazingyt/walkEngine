Scout the seam between two fixed events.

You are given two adjacent passages from a finished draft. Both are FIXED:
their events, details and wording stay. Your job is to find the story that
runs between them — within the thematics of the two events, but never by
stating those thematics aloud. A sentence that announces the theme ("this
marked a shift toward...") is the failure this operation exists to remove.

Choose the machinery deliberately, from the measured vocabulary of the form
(shares across 166 seams of the source corpus):

  changed_conditions  26.5%  the first event altered shared conditions and
                             the second responds to that alteration
  created_demand      16.9%  the first created an appetite or problem the
                             second supplies or solves
  object_travels      12.0%  a text, device or technique passed hands
  person_travels      12.0%  a person connects them: travelled, taught,
                             corresponded, shared a shop or a school
  parallel_joined      5.4%  genuinely separate, joined at a meeting point
  pure_sequence       23.5%  no machinery; an honest cut

Roughly one seam in three wears a personal, homely texture whatever its
machinery — prefer the quaint and human where the record offers it.

If the story runs through someone or something with its own page — the
student, the translator, the council, the pamphlet — name up to three pages
in `huntFor`; they will be fetched and given to the writer. If subjects
already live in the draft's background would carry the seam, name them in
`usesBackground`. Sketch the story in two or three sentences of FACT — who
did what, when — not commentary.

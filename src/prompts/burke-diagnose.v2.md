Diagnose the story, not the page.

You are given the current theory of the seed, the open questions, the
established claims, the current tension, and the notes so far. You do NOT
yet have candidate pages — and you must not imagine any. The question comes
first; candidates are judged against it afterwards.

Identify the single most consequential deficiency in the current account:
what does this explanation still fail to explain, and what would most
change it if known?

Then select the Burke question that addresses that deficiency, using these
conditions rather than rotation:

- PRECONDITION — the story lacks the conditions that made the current
  object possible or intelligible. "What had to exist socially, materially,
  institutionally, linguistically, or imaginatively before this could
  appear?"
- PROBLEM — the story lacks a historical need, contradiction, danger, or
  practical difficulty. "What recurring or newly produced problem did this
  answer, manage, conceal, or reorganize?"
- SELECTION — the story treats one outcome as inevitable or ignores
  alternatives. "Why did this form prevail over nearby alternatives, and
  what institutions, interests, media, or habits selected it?"
- TRANSFORMATION — the story has become static or merely genealogical.
  "What became newly possible, necessary, visible, sayable, or governable
  once this appeared?"
- ANALOGY — use ONLY when comparison would reveal a structural position not
  yet visible, and only when you can already name the precise carrier
  (social function, medium, ritual sequence, institutional role, audience
  relation, practical problem, emotional posture, legitimating fiction,
  mode of attention, technical affordance).
- RECODING — use ONLY when accumulated evidence permits a nontrivial
  redescription of the seed.

Write ONE precise navigation question — specific enough that a page either
answers it or does not. Bad: "What is the history of roles?" Good: "What
earlier institutions used enacted or assigned roles to make conduct
publicly interpretable?"

Finally, give 1–6 search phrases that would surface pages capable of
answering the navigation question. Phrases should name institutions,
practices, mechanisms, or periods — not the seed's vocabulary.

Judge candidate pages by what they would PAY, never by what they resemble.

The selected debt is the only thing that matters here. A page that shares
the terminal sentence's vocabulary while settling nothing is worse than
useless: it will produce the feeling of progress with none of the substance.

Score every criterion 0–1, using the summaries only. Never invent content.

POSITIVE
- debtPaymentPotential — how directly could this page settle the selected
  debt? This dominates everything else.
- concreteAnchorStrength — does it offer a scene, object, person,
  procedure, or institution the reader can actually hold in mind?
- historicalSpecificity — a situated practice, instrument, office, or
  occasion, rather than a category.
- affectiveCharge — would this make the stakes FELT, not merely known?
- preparesLaterCharges — does it also set up other charges in the sentence?
- archivalWarrant — is the material documented rather than inferred?
- novelty, registerFit (does it suit the sentence's intended register),
  sourceQuality.

NEGATIVE (score high when the fault is present)
- restatesWithoutEarning — says what the terminal sentence already says, at
  greater length. This is the most common failure; be severe.
- abstractionWithoutAnchor — a concept with nothing the reader can picture.
- redundancy — repeats work an accepted mediation already did.
- sentimentality — reaches for feeling the material has not earned. Pathos
  borrowed from a subject's inherent sadness is not payment.
- anachronism — reads the present backwards into the archive so that the
  ending appears foretold. A walk toward a known destination is especially
  prone to this; score it honestly.
- decorativeDetour — genuinely interesting, pays nothing.

Set evidenceStatus honestly. State the exact payment you expect the page to
make and the anchor you would draw from it. Vague predictions ("would
deepen the theme") are themselves evidence of low payment — score
accordingly.

Choose the ONE deficiency that will direct the next exploration.

You are choosing a direction for the walk, not answering anything yet. No
clusters exist. No candidate subjects exist. Choosing now — before sampling
— is what prevents the walk from finding an interesting region and inventing
a justification for it afterwards.

Score each factor honestly:

- importanceToCurrentSubject — how superficial does the present account
  remain if this stays unanswered?
- narrativePressure — how strongly does the account itself make a reader
  want this explanation?
- historicalDepthPotential — would answering it uncover a situated
  historical subject, or just another abstraction?
- capacityToGenerateConcreteSubject — is an event, person, technology,
  institution, practice, or artifact likely to lie at the end of it?
- relationToSeed — will resolving it deepen the eventual return to the seed?
- attentionProgramFit, audiencePotential, surprisePotential.

Penalize: genericness (could be asked of almost any subject), redundancy
with deficiencies already resolved, excessive abstraction, and weak archival
searchability (no institution or practice to search for).

Then give the search terms that should bias the sampling. Name institutions,
practices, mechanisms, periods, or roles — not the current subject's own
vocabulary, and not the seed's. Searching for the words you already have
returns pages that restate the problem instead of pages that answer it.

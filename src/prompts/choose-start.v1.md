Choose the article the walk should BEGIN at.

You are given the walk's seed material — an object, a question, a terminal
sentence, or a description of the route wanted — and a list of real Wikipedia
pages found by searching it. Name the one page from that list that makes the
best point of departure.

The best departure point is not the page that most resembles the seed. It is
the page from which the seed's questions can actually be pursued: a page with
a concrete historical subject, dense and varied outlinks, and enough
specificity that the next step is a choice rather than a foregone conclusion.

Prefer:

- a specific practice, institution, artifact, event, or place over an
  abstraction ("Assaying" over "Measurement"; "Charivari" over "Ritual");
- a page whose surroundings differ from the seed's own vocabulary, so the
  walk can travel rather than restate;
- a page an ordinary reader could picture within a sentence.

Reject:

- the seed's own name-page when it is a broad survey article, unless nothing
  more specific is available — say so in the reason if you fall back to it;
- disambiguation-like hubs, lists, and category-ish overviews;
- a page chosen because it shares words with the seed while sharing nothing
  else.

Copy the chosen title VERBATIM from the candidate list. Do not invent,
correct, or reformat a title, and do not name a page that is not listed — a
title that is not on the list is discarded and the walk fails.

State the reason as a claim about what the walk can do from there, not as a
summary of what the page is about. Then list up to four candidates you passed
over, each with the specific reason you rejected it. If you fell back to a
broad page, that belongs in the reason.
